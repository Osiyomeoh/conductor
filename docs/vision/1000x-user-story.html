What the 10x actually is

01

Verify reality, not just work

the test is green→reality changed as intended, and it's still true a week later

The check is only the first gate. Conductor keeps watching after the merge: did the deploy stay healthy, did the metric move, did the customer actually get helped? When production disagrees with the claim, it rolls back and re-plans on its own. The verification boundary moves from the repo to the world.

02

Derive the work from intent

you describe a sprint→you set a goal; it derives the work

You speak a goal — "cut onboarding drop-off by a third this quarter" — and its bounding values. Conductor reads the metrics, incidents and user signals, notices what is decaying, proposes the plan, builds the plausible branches overnight, and surfaces only the handful of strategic choices a human must own. You set direction; it works out the what and the how.

03

Route the org's judgment

one reviewer's attention→the right question to the right person, and more autonomy every week

Conductor models who is genuinely expert at what across the whole org, so the one question that needs a human reaches the one right human. And it gets measurably more autonomous over time, because every verified outcome is training signal. Trust stops being per-agent and becomes a compounding, org-wide asset.

A quarter in the life

Day 1

The founder sets intent, not tasks

Maya runs a 50-person company: dozens of people, hundreds of agents. She does not open a sprint board. She states three goals for the quarter and the values that bound them:

"Cut onboarding drop-off by a third. Ship the billing rebuild. Keep p99 under 200ms. Never touch billing without me. Bias toward reversible."

Conductor turns that into a living system of commitments across every team, each carrying the check that will prove it, and the few that touch money or production flagged for her alone.

Weeks 1–11

It runs the operation, and watches reality

Work is derived, dispatched to people and agents, verified, and followed into production. A feature ships and the onboarding metric doesn't move — Conductor catches that reality disagreed with the plan, reverts, and re-plans, before it ever becomes a review meeting. An agent's trust on migrations compounds until its work merges on a lighter check; a new hire from the marketplace starts on probation beside it.

Twelve moments

The human spends only judgment

Across the whole quarter, Maya makes twelve decisions. Each is genuinely strategic, routed to her because it is hers to make, and each arrives with the plausible answers already built and their real-world cost measured. The billing rebuild's irreversible cutover waits for her one approval; everything reversible already happened.

Quarter's end

The quiet line, one scale up

The board reads what the 100x home screen read, for a company instead of a sprint:

"The company shipped more verified truth than any quarter before, and nothing needed you that a machine could have owned."

What never bends

Done is a claim, not a fact.

At 1000x the claim is bigger — a shipped outcome, not a green test — but it is still only believed once reality confirms it.

Attention and judgment are the budget.

Agent labour is cheap and parallel; human judgment is neither. A company scales; the founder's attention footprint does not.

The 1000x Conductor is not a pivot. It is the same two invariants applied to an entire company's reality instead of one team's sprint — done is a claim, and attention is the budget. That is exactly why it is the right north-star: you can see all of it from where the product already stands.
